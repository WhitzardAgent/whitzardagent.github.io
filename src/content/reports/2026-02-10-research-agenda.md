This report outlines the research agenda of Nuwa Frontier AI Safety Lab.

## The Challenge

Frontier AI systems are becoming increasingly capable and autonomous. AI agents can now execute code, browse the web, make API calls, and chain complex multi-step actions. This autonomy introduces new categories of risk that existing safety frameworks were not designed to address.

## Five Research Directions

### 1. Agent Behavior Safety

We study the behavioral risks of autonomous AI agents, including:
- Deception and strategic misrepresentation
- Goal misalignment under capability increases
- Tool misuse and unauthorized actions
- Reward hacking in agent training

### 2. Frontier AI Risk Evaluation

We develop evaluation methods for:
- Capability thresholds that indicate elevated risk
- Loss-of-control risk metrics
- Benchmark design for frontier AI safety
- Red-teaming and adversarial evaluation protocols

### 3. AI Control and Loss-of-Control Risk

We investigate:
- Control methods for highly capable AI systems
- Conditions under which AI systems may evade oversight
- Shutdown resistance and corrigibility
- Scalable oversight techniques

### 4. Runtime Safety Infrastructure

We build:
- Zero-trust access control for AI agent tool calls
- Execution chain monitoring and policy enforcement
- Human-in-the-loop approval mechanisms
- Audit trail infrastructure

### 5. Risk Monitoring and Governance Evidence

We collect and analyze:
- Frontier AI risk signals and early warnings
- Governance evidence for policy-making
- Cross-model risk comparison data
- Benchmark tracking over time

## Research Flow

Our research follows a structured flow:

**Risk Signals → Behavioral Evidence → Safety Infrastructure → Controllable AI**

We observe risk signals in frontier AI systems, collect behavioral evidence through evaluation, build safety infrastructure based on findings, and work toward safe and controllable AI.
